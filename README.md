# resume-screener

Candidate–job matching: given a job description, rank a corpus of resumes by
fit. A bi-encoder (two-tower) neural retriever trained with a listwise
ranking loss, served behind a FastAPI screening endpoint, evaluated against
an honest BM25 keyword baseline on the same held-out split.

**Status: synthetic-data demo.** All resumes, job descriptions, and
relevance labels are generated by `data_gen.py` (seed 42). No real resumes,
companies, or people appear anywhere. Nothing here is deployed; there are no
users.

## Why this exists

I work in HR/recruiting: 25–40 open staff requisitions at a time, ~5,000
resumes per requisition. Keyword-based ATS screening misses strong
candidates who phrase skills differently ("ML" vs "machine learning") and
can't infer competence ("PyTorch" implies "machine learning"). This project
asks whether a learned semantic retriever can do better than keyword search
at the actual task: ranking candidates by skill coverage of a requisition.

## How it works

- **Synthetic corpus** (`data_gen.py`): 12 role families × 150 job
  descriptions (1,800 total) and 250 resumes per family (3,000 total).
  Surface forms vary independently between JDs and resumes (e.g. a JD may
  say "ML" while a resume says "machine learning"), with a mild skew (JDs
  favor terse forms, resumes favor spelled-out forms) to mimic real
  vocabulary mismatch. A documented implication graph (`IMPLICATIONS`)
  models inferred competence: e.g. listing "PyTorch" implies
  "machine learning" / "deep learning" even when those words never appear.
- **Model** (`models.py`): two-tower bi-encoder. Token embeddings (unigrams
  + bigrams) → learned attention pooling → MLP → L2-normalized 128-d
  vectors; cosine similarity retrieval. Separate embedding tables per tower.
  CPU-friendly: trains in minutes, no transformers.
- **Training** (`train.py`): ListNet listwise loss over stratified resume
  lists per JD (high/medium/low/zero skill overlap) plus an auxiliary
  multi-label skill-prediction loss that forces the embeddings to encode
  *which* skills a text mentions. Skill heads are discarded at serving time;
  inference uses raw text only.
- **Relevance** (`evaluate.py::relevant_resumes`): a resume is relevant to
  a JD iff it shares the role family **and** covers ≥4 of the JD's required
  skills (explicit or implied). Fine-grained, not family-level.
- **Baseline** (`evaluate.py::BM25Index`): honest BM25 (k1=1.5, b=0.75) over
  the full resume texts, same held-out JDs, same relevance definition.
- **Serving** (`serving/`): FastAPI `POST /screen` (by `job_id` or raw
  `job_text`) → ranked candidates; `GET /health`, `GET /metrics`.
  Startup precomputes all resume embeddings; each request is one job-tower
  forward pass + exact top-k cosine retrieval.

## Results (measured, 360 held-out JDs)

| model      | Recall@10 | Recall@50 | NDCG@10 | MRR   |
|------------|-----------|-----------|---------|-------|
| BM25       | 0.0820    | 0.3298    | 0.8034  | 0.9329 |
| bi-encoder | 0.0725    | 0.3097    | 0.7292  | 0.8806 |

Serving (local, 1 uvicorn worker, 50 sequential + 20 concurrent requests):
mean 31.7 ms, p95 42.7 ms sequential; 33.6 req/s at 20 concurrent
(see `serving/load_results.json`).

Reproduce: `./run_all.sh` (uses `.venv/bin/python` if present), then
`python serving/load_test.py`.

## What the numbers say (honest read)

The bi-encoder slightly **underperforms** BM25 on every metric. That is a
real result of the final trained model, not an artifact, and it is worth
understanding why rather than tuning it away:

- **The benchmark favors term matching by construction.** Relevance is
  defined as skill-overlap counting, and synthetic resumes list skills as
  explicit keywords. BM25 gets exact matches on the JD's required skills;
  the 65/35 surface-form mismatch and the implication graph give the neural
  model room to help, but not enough to beat a strong keyword baseline on
  clean data. On messier real resumes with heavier paraphrase, the ranking
  could easily flip -- this benchmark can't tell us that.
- **Recall@10 is near its ceiling for both models.** With ~103 relevant
  resumes per JD on average, the maximum achievable recall@10 is ~0.097;
  both models sit at ~75-85% of that ceiling. MRR and NDCG@10 are the
  discriminative metrics here.
- **The small DAN is not a transformer.** 128-d attention-pooled
  embeddings trained on weak synthetic supervision close most of the gap
  to BM25 (MRR 0.881 vs 0.933) but don't beat it. That's an honest,
  expected outcome for this model class -- and exactly the kind of result
  that should make you skeptical of "AI beats keywords" claims without a
  baseline.

During this build the training had two genuine bugs that were fixed (both
documented in `train.py`): the ListNet target was normalized in a way that
flattened it to near-uniform, so the model learned almost no score
separation (MRR 0.09); and the training lists contained only same-family
resumes while the eval ranks all 3,000, so cross-family outliers dominated
top-1 until cross-family negatives were added. The numbers above are from
the fixed, honestly trained model -- no hyperparameter tuning was done to
chase BM25.

## Limitations

- Synthetic benchmark; real resumes are messier and relevance is fuzzier.
  The keyword-friendly construction here likely understates the neural
  model's edge on real data -- but that edge is unmeasured, not claimed.
- The bi-encoder is a small DAN, not a transformer; don't expect SOTA.
- Relevance is skill-overlap-countable, which favors term-matching
  baselines; the implication/synonym structure is where semantics help.
- Training uses weak synthetic supervision (skill-overlap lists); no
  human relevance judgments anywhere.
- No fairness auditing, no PII handling, no production hardening — this is
  a modeling demo, not a hiring product.

## Layout

```
data_gen.py      synthetic JD/resume corpus + split
dataset.py       tokenization (unigrams+bigrams), vocab, tensors
models.py        two-tower bi-encoder with attention pooling
train.py         ListNet + auxiliary skill-prediction training
evaluate.py      BM25 baseline, metrics, held-out evaluation
screen.py        CLI screening demo
serving/         FastAPI app, loader, offline eval, load test, Dockerfile
tests/           pytest suite
```
